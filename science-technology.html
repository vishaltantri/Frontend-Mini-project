Exercise 03 Text formatting Sub / Sup

Science and Technology

A short primer for the Science Club, using the formatting tags that make technical writing readable.

Where curiosity meets craft

Science asks the questions that move us forward, while technology turns the answers into tools people can use. Innovation usually happens in the space between the two — a careful experiment on one side, a prototype that ships on the other. Scientific Research gives us the evidence to choose the right path, and an open, questioning culture is essential if that evidence is going to reach the people who need it.

Every laboratory on campus depends on the same loop: observe, test, measure and share. The periodic table, the physics lab and the software studio all speak the same language of reproducible results. That is why even a simple formula deserves to be written clearly.

Chemical formulaCO2
Algebraic identitya3 + b3
Einstein’s relationE = mc2

Carbon dioxide, CO2, is the gas behind both photosynthesis and the climate conversation. In algebra, a3 + b3 expands to (a + b)(a2 − ab + b2) — a neat reminder that notation is part of the explanation.

Tags used on this page

<b> Science — bold emphasis
<i> Innovation — italic emphasis
<u> Scientific Research — underline
<mark> essential — highlight
<sub> CO₂ — chemical subscript
<sup> a³, mc² — mathematical superscript

What this page demonstrates

  • Main heading “Science and Technology”
  • Introductory paragraph about the field
  • Bold Science, italic Innovation
  • Underlined Scientific Research
  • Highlighted essential
  • CO₂ with sub, a³ + b³ with sup
